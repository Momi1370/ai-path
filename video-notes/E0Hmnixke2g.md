**What it teaches.** A fast tour of the main algorithms in one picture. Chapter 13 of the notes is built on it.

**Examples the notes take from it**
- Supervised: nearest neighbours; support vector machines (separating cats from elephants by weight and nose length); naive Bayes (the spam filter); decision trees (heart-attack risk); random forests and boosting (many trees); neural networks (recognizing handwritten digits).
- Unsupervised: k-means clustering (emails grouped into three unnamed groups) and PCA (fish length and height become one "shape" feature).
- Each algorithm has a typical use. Pick one by trying a simple baseline first and measuring on held-out data.

**Remember.** Supervised needs labels; unsupervised finds structure. For tables of data, trees and boosting are the usual first choices.
