**What it teaches.** A formal definition of unsupervised learning, and two types besides clustering.

**Key points**
- Supervised data has inputs X and labels Y; unsupervised data has only X.
- Anomaly detection finds unusual events, which matters for fraud detection in finance.
- Dimensionality reduction squeezes a big data set into fewer numbers while losing as little as possible.
- A quiz in the video sorts four examples: spam filtering and diagnosing diabetes are supervised (labels exist); grouping news articles and market segmentation are unsupervised.

**Remember.** Ask one question: do I have the right answers for the training examples? If yes, supervised; if no, unsupervised.
