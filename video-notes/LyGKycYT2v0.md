**What it teaches.** What the dot product really means, which explains why a neuron computes w . x.

**Key points**
- The numerical recipe: multiply matching coordinates and add. The geometric picture: project one vector onto the other and multiply the lengths.
- The surprising fact is that the two views agree. The reason is duality: a vector with 2 numbers is equivalent to a linear transformation from the plane to the number line, written as a 1 by 2 matrix.
- Applying that transformation to a vector is computationally identical to taking a dot product with the vector. A unit vector's dot product is the projection onto its span; scaling the vector scales the result.
- So a vector can be seen as the embodiment of a transformation, not only an arrow.

**Remember.** w . x measures how much x points in the direction of w, which is why it works as a score.
